3. WHAT THE NEW DATA SHOWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(a) "Wide-but-short" tiles (m=256, n=64) are a third small-n spill class.
    Across A2/A4 the (256,64) tile produces a 1.24x-1.25x same-tile speedup;
    A3 (which narrows to i8 -- 4x not 2x) gives 1.20x at the same tile. The
    mechanism mirrors the (128,128) / (64,256) cache-hit wins already in the
    report. The narrowed cat shrinks the post-cat tile and ptxas can keep
    more working set in registers; previously we missed this entire column
    of the heatmap.

(b) A5 has a second large rescue at (256,64): 5.13x. The known A5 rescue
    is 5.69x at (64,256). The (256,64) cell sits one column over and shows
    the same pattern: at 256 elements per inner dim with 4 cloned narrow
    chains, the OFF kernel exceeds 255 regs and spills heavily (19.7 ms
    per launch); the narrowed ON chain stays in registers (3.8 ms). The
    rescue is real and reproducible in two non-square tile shapes for A5.

(c) The largest tiles (256,256) are uniformly NO-OP across A2/A3/A4/A5.
    At (256,256) every kernel spills heavily in BOTH OFF and ON; the
    register-pressure gate is engaged for the rewrite candidates that
    would still spill post-narrowing. This matches the gate's design.

(d) (64,128) and (128,64) at A2/A4 are essentially neutral (0.98x-1.01x).
    These are tile shapes where neither state spills and the SASS is
    similar. Expected.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. NOTES ON METHODOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(a) The pass-toggle was via env var, not the sed approach. This is the
    fix mentioned in A2_bvb_investigation.txt: the old sed targeted
    compiler.py line 243, which moved to a wrapper with 12-space indent
    after the register-pressure gate was wired in. Using
    TRITON_DISABLE_DOWNCAST_REORDER_OPTIMIZER guarantees the toggle takes effect.

(b) Per-tile timing comes from nsys cuda_gpu_kern_sum (the same protocol
    as the original sweep), 100 iterations between cudaProfilerStart and
    cudaProfilerStop, 3 warmup launches discarded. avg_ns column of the
    CSV is what the speedup table uses.

(c) The register-pressure gate is ACTIVE in this run. For the heaviest
    tiles where the gate refuses (e.g., A2 (256,256), A4 (256,256), and
    A5 (256,256)), the OFF and ON IR are identical and the per-launch
    times match within noise. This is exactly the gate's intended
    behavior and now appears as a row of 1.00x cells in the heatmap.

(d) Bit-exact correctness was NOT re-verified for the new tiles in this
    sweep (the original A1-A7 verification covered the 5 sampled tiles).
    If the heatmap data is to be a citable claim, a SHA-256 pass over
    every output at every new tile should be added. The mechanism the
    pass uses is identical to the existing 5 tiles; no new code paths
    are exercised.

7. ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY

The fill-in sweep closes the 16 missing cells in Final_Report Figure 11
(Patterns 6-9 at the 4 tiles the original sweep skipped). Most missing
cells are neutral (1.00x), but four reveal real wins: a (256,64) cluster

disclaimer; and the diamond-family B-v-B range becomes 1.06x-1.24x. The
gate's behavior under heavy spill is empirically confirmed: every (256,
256) cell is 1.00x.
